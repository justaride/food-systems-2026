title: "Iceland Needs Emergency Food Stockpiles, Say University Researchers"

Just as squirrels stash nuts before winter, Icelanders might want to start doing the same.

The University of Iceland has handed over a detailed report on emergency food planning to the [Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation](https://www.government.is/ministries/ministry-of-culture-innovation-and-higher-education/). The research project—which quietly started last year—wrapped up on 30 June with a checklist for how to keep the nation fed during tough times.

As [reported by RÚV](https://www.ruv.is/frettir/innlent/2025-07-07-faeduoryggi-islands-skodad-til-lengri-tima-neydarbirgdir-skipta-skopum-447876), the aim of the project is to prepare Iceland for situations like natural disasters, geopolitical turmoil, or sudden disruptions to imports.

## Red Cross recommends

Based on [Red Cross](https://www.icelandreview.com/news/red-cross-responds-to-grindavik-funding-concerns/) guidelines, it recommends that households keep enough supplies to last at least three days, but also highlights the importance of long-term planning — extending to three weeks, three months, or even six months.

Researchers assessed Iceland’s food systems, import dependence, and domestic production. Finland’s preparedness efforts were used as a model, as it is considered the most advanced in the Nordic region in terms of food security planning.

Professor Ólöf Guðný Geirsdóttir from the [University of Iceland](https://www.icelandreview.com/news/icelands-largest-university-celebrates-2025-graduates/) noted that while the country is self-sufficient in protein, including meat and fish, grain must be imported.

“Icelandic grain production amounts to about 1% of what can be used for food production,” she stated in the report.

She also stressed the importance of contingency plans for food infrastructure, especially since Iceland’s only grain mill was dismantled earlier this year. The need to support vulnerable populations was also mentioned, including infants and those with allergies or special dietary needs.
