On LinkedIn , founder and CEO Sorosh Tavakoli said the decline in the plant-based category in recent years had made it almost impossible for the company to grow. He explained that Stockeld had been preparing for another fundraise, but had decided to &#8220;close in a responsible way&#8221; after realizing the company did not have enough momentum to justify further capital. Stockeld&#8217;s team has stayed on to support the winding down of the company, which has included selling equipment, closing offices and labs, and selling any remaining inventory. The brand&#8217;s products will be removed from shelves and menus in the coming months. &#8220;Of everything we built, the culture is what I’m most proud of,&#8221; said Tavakoli. &#8220;It brought out the best in us &#8212; a unique combination of trust, creativity, and joy, even in hard times. It has been a privilege to experience that kind of authenticity and flow together.&#8221; Image: Sorosh Tavakoli on LinkedIn &#8220;We built something real&#8221; Stockeld Dreamery achieved considerable success over the years, with its plant-based cheeses sold at bagel shops, burger joints, and retail locations internationally. These included PLNT Burger , Mr. Charlie&#8217;s , Ess-a-Bagel , and over 60 New York City bodegas . Stockeld&#8217;s products included a cultured cream cheese made from lentils and chickpeas (available in three flavors) and cultured slices that were claimed to melt twice as fast as any other vegan cheese (sold in Cheddar and Provolone varieties). The company is now having conversations about a new home for its IP, and welcomes communications from anyone who may be interested. &#8220;We knew success would demand something extraordinary and we came close, but as the market fell, it just got so much harder than we ever expected,&#8221; said Tavakoli. &#8220;All in all, it’s bittersweet to let go. But I’m proud we built something real, with purpose, integrity, and joy.&#8221; Don't miss out!
