## What is controlled

- Public procurement is a plausible demand-side transition lever, especially where public kitchens buy repeated volumes.
- Copenhagen is useful as a benchmark because the mechanism is not just procurement text; it combines political targets, kitchen skills, menu planning, seasonality, waste work, and food-from-scratch capability.
- Norway lacks a clean national public-organic food-procurement share comparable to the stronger Danish/Swedish/Finnish series.
- The staged annual procurement series includes differentiated metrics, for example Finland's public-kitchen daily organic-use survey and Swedish public-sector organic purchase share/value rows.

## Pilotable question

Can one municipality or public buyer implement a procurement standard that increases sustainable/local/organic demand while protecting budgets through kitchen training, menu redesign, seasonal planning, and waste reduction?

## Data and model inputs

- `organic_public_procurement_annual.csv` for comparable Nordic indicators and caveats.
- Public-procurement analysis notes on Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Norway, and data gaps.
- Whitepaper focus-area notes on Copenhagen, Oslo, and why mechanism matters more than the organic headline alone.
- Potential local procurement inputs:
  - annual food spend by institution type;
  - supplier concentration and contract structure;
  - meal counts and menu categories;
  - waste baseline by kitchen;
  - staff training needs;
  - small-lot thresholds and local-supplier participation constraints.

## Decision options for the workshop

- **Municipal pilot standard:** write a practical standard with required measurement fields and a kitchen-support package.
- **School-meal demand track:** evaluate whether universal or expanded school meals would create a stable demand engine.
- **Data-first track:** start by measuring Norwegian public-food spend and organic/local/sustainable shares before setting targets.
- **Benchmark-only:** keep Copenhagen/Sweden/Finland as roadmap references without launching a Norwegian pilot.

## Human validation asks

- Ask municipal kitchens which requirements are feasible without extra budget.
- Ask procurement lawyers how to express local/sustainable criteria within procurement rules.
- Ask finance reviewers whether cost neutrality should be a pilot condition or a monitored hypothesis.
- Ask nutrition/public-health reviewers whether the standard should prioritize organic share, local supply, meal quality, waste, or all four with weights.

## Do not say

- Do not say Copenhagen's result came from procurement clauses alone.
- Do not claim Norway has a national public-organic procurement baseline unless a source is added.
- Do not treat organic share as a complete sustainability metric.
- Do not assume public procurement can bypass logistics and supplier-capacity constraints.
