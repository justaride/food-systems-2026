The dominant productionist paradigm emphasizes aggregate production volumes, a focus on a few key products, and the dominant role of large exporting countries and transnational corporations. New dietary patterns, climate change issues, and the perception of food as a commodity - just to cite a few - have resulted in a growing number of conflicting ideas regarding how to produce, dis- tribute, sell, and consume food. Lifestyles—the combined interests, opinions, behaviours, and behavioural orientations of indi- viduals, groups, and cultures—are also changing rapidly. Every day, Nordic citizens connect with local, national, and global food systems when they choose, buy, cook, and consume food. Every day, Nordic countries import food and fertil- iser and export some of their local production. Each Nordic country has a distinct food system, yet all Nordic countries share common strengths and challenges. Nordic countries benefit from a safe and plentiful food supply, strong social and institutional foun- dations, high levels of innovation and multi-sector collaboration. At the same time, large portions of the population suffer from unhealthy diets, which lead to poor health and environmental damage across the region. Despite much work having been done to improve the food system, Nordic countries still experience a substantial gap between current and desired food systems. For example, the environmental impact of the Nordic population’s food consumption is three times the climate boundary and twice the land use boundary. Therefore, actions towards a consistent transfor- mation of the Nordic food systems are needed. The main final objective should be to establish an integrated food systems approach that aligns agri- cultural and aquatic food production, trade, manu- facturing, retailing and consumption priorities, and that contributes to healthier, more sustainable, equitable, and resilient food systems, in line with the Nordic commitment to Agenda 2030, the Paris Climate Agreement, and the EU Farm to Fork Strat- egy. 11 Intervention nr. 1 Strengthen coordination among public and private stakeholders to support national and inter-coun- try pathways for Nordic food sys- tems transformation The current Nordic governance structure for food In the Nordic countries, an increasing number of systems is fragmented, as it has grown over time regions and cities are promoting multi-stakeholder in the absence of a systems lens.
