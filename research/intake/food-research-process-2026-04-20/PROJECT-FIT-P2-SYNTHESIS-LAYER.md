## 1. Side-stream valorization and redistribution

### What the first-wave now lets us say

The strongest change in this P2 round is on food-waste hierarchy and side-stream handling.

The batch now supports a much firmer synthesis that:

- prevention should stay above downstream treatment in the circular hierarchy
- redistribution is not a marginal add-on, but a real system lever
- supplier-retailer interface design can structurally create overproduction and surplus
- chain-level coordination is necessary if waste reduction is to move beyond isolated actor initiatives

This is the clearest area where the new evidence materially changes the whitepaper's footing.

### Why the footing is better

`Brancoli 2021` is especially useful because it ties bread surplus directly to take-back arrangements and the supplier-retail interface, which is exactly the kind of mechanism-level explanation the earlier pack lacked.

`Mäkelä 2023` adds a coordination lens across supply chains rather than a single-company lens.

`Albizzati 2021` gives a stronger ranking logic for intervention hierarchy by showing why prevention and redistribution should be prioritized over lower-value treatment routes.

`Food Redistribution in the Nordic Region` and `Matsvinnutvalgets anbefalinger` give the Nordic and Norwegian policy scaffolding around those same operational findings.

### Publication-safe synthesis

The current corpus now supports a cautious but credible claim that side-stream valorization and redistribution should be treated as structured coordination and governance questions, not only as consumer-awareness or isolated rescue initiatives.

### Remaining limit

What is still thinner is:

- Norway-specific implementation metrics at system scale
- hard evidence on actual redistribution capacity bottlenecks
- operator-by-operator economics for scaling redistribution and valorization pathways

## 2. Nutrient loops and circular input recovery

### What the first-wave now lets us say

The batch no longer has to treat nutrient loops as a nearly empty theme.

It now supports a narrower but defensible claim that:

- technical loop pathways exist
- some of them reach beyond abstract circularity rhetoric
- social acceptance and market uptake are real constraints, not afterthoughts

### Why the footing is better

The digestate greenhouse paper gives direct proof-of-concept for a `food to waste to food` pathway rather than only a general circularity aspiration.

The fish-sludge willingness-to-pay paper is important because it introduces the demand-side barrier that technical-loop papers often ignore.

The cascade-processing review broadens the frame by connecting food, feed, biomaterials, and energy pathways in one biomass logic.

### Publication-safe synthesis

The evidence base now supports the statement that nutrient-loop and circular-input pathways are technically credible and analytically relevant, but that they still face unresolved barriers around acceptance, integration, and system-scale deployment.

### Remaining limit

The main gaps are still:

- Norwegian scale evidence
- quality assurance and regulatory treatment in practice
- cost and logistics evidence for deployment in existing supply chains

The highest-value next pickup here is still:

- `NORSUS - OR 48.23 Biorest fra marine råstoffer og husdyrgjødsel (2024)`

## 3. Alternative feed and marine / biomass substitution

### What the first-wave now lets us say

This theme has moved from `mostly hypothetical in this batch` to `credible transition field with concrete anchors`.

The current pack now supports a careful synthesis that:

- a Nordic research and commercialization field does exist around alternative proteins and feed substitution
- regulatory permissions and category boundaries matter materially
- incumbent feed and marine actors are part of the transition picture, not outside it
- circular feed substitution should be read as an industrial-ecosystem question, not only a startup question

### Why the footing is better

`The Nordic alternative protein research ecosystem - GFI Europe` gives field structure.

The EU insect novel-food item gives a minimum regulatory baseline.

`BioMar` and `Hailia` help connect transition talk to actual feed and marine-system actors.

The cascade-processing source is again useful because it links food and feed substitution inside one resource logic.

### Publication-safe synthesis

The current corpus supports the view that alternative feed and circular marine / biomass substitution are no longer peripheral ideas in the Nordic transition field, but the evidence is still stronger on ecosystem direction, regulation, and actor positioning than on proven large-scale displacement of incumbent input systems.

### Remaining limit

Still missing for stronger claims:

- displacement data
- adoption volumes
- cost competitiveness at scale
- Norway-specific integration evidence in established feed chains

## 4. Fermentation, mycoprotein, and scale-up barriers

### What the first-wave now lets us say

This is the gap that improved least in terms of hard proof, but it still improved in an important way.

The first-wave now supports a more grounded statement that:

- fermentation and mycoprotein are credible transition domains
- public policy can actively shape the space
- Nordic ecosystem formation is real
- but current first-wave evidence does not justify a strong claim that commercial scale-up is already succeeding system-wide

### Why the footing is better

`Danish Action Plan for Plant-based Foods` is the strongest policy-side anchor in this theme.

`GFI Europe` makes the ecosystem legible.

`Enifer` gives at least one live industrial pathway that is closer to production reality than a generic landing page.

### Publication-safe synthesis

The corpus now supports the claim that fermentation and mycoprotein belong in the transition layer of the whitepaper, but not yet in the part of the argument that claims demonstrated system-scale impact.

### Remaining limit

What is still weak in the current first-wave set:

- commercialization outcomes
- capacity and unit-economics evidence
- hard evidence on scaling constraints in Nordic operations

That means the scale-barrier story should still rely on a second-wave pull of failure and restructuring cases if it is to become a major section.
