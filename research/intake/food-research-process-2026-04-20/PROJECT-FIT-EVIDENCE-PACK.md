## Purpose

This pack integrates the three track notes into one working backbone for Food Systems 2026.

The goal is not to restate every document in full. The detailed per-source writeups are already in the track notes. This pack does three things:

1. normalizes the 34-source shortlist into one evidence register
2. clarifies what is `narrative priority` versus what is `evidence strength`
3. identifies the cross-track mechanisms that should structure the next synthesis pass

## How to read the pack

`tier` means narrative priority in the June whitepaper workflow:

- `immediate-core`: should be loaded first
- `core-support`: should be pulled in after the first pass

`confidence` means evidentiary strength of the current writeup, not importance:

- `high`: strong fit and well-grounded in official material or strong local structured summaries
- `medium`: useful and usable, but more caveated or built from thinner support
- `low`: contextual or advocacy-heavy; do not use alone

`evidence_basis` means where the current summary mainly comes from:

- `summary-led`: built mainly from strong local structured summaries
- `document-led`: built mainly from the intake document itself
- `summary-plus-spot-check`: local summary plus selective PDF checking
- `mixed`: partial local summary support plus adjacent contextual material
- `contextual`: useful for framing, but not a strong standalone evidence block

## Core synthesis

### 1. The decisive control points are access infrastructure and coordination layers

Across all three tracks, the strongest shared finding is that Food Systems 2026 should not frame the problem only as farm output, consumer price, or isolated circular pilots.

The key control points sit in:

- contracts and supplier terms
- wholesale and logistics infrastructure
- store access and premises access
- data and demand coordination
- emergency distribution and operational preparedness

That is the main reason the policy, operator, and resilience tracks belong together.

### 2. Market structure and resilience are the same system viewed from different angles

The policy-market sources show concentrated bargaining power and weak access for smaller producers.

The operator sources show how that power is made real through fleets, warehouses, store networks, supplier governance, and integrated value-chain control.

The resilience sources show why those same structures matter in crises: a system can be efficient and profitable while still being brittle, concentrated, and hard to adapt under stress.

### 3. Self-sufficiency is necessary but insufficient as a framing device

Several of the best resilience sources show that headline self-sufficiency percentages are too crude on their own.

Food Systems 2026 should instead use a wider resilience frame that includes:

- feed and input dependence
- logistics and payment systems
- storage and distribution capacity
- producer incentives
- biological robustness in marine systems
- the ability of local and regional actors to stay viable

### 4. The base batch is strongest on governance, operators, and preparedness, but the first-wave P2 extension now adds a usable transition layer

This matters strategically.

The base pack gives a strong foundation for:

- market structure
- governance reform
- producer access
- operator control points
- preparedness and self-sufficiency

The first-wave P2 extension now adds usable footing on:

- side-stream valorization and redistribution
- nutrient-loop pathways
- alternative feed and marine / biomass substitution
- fermentation and mycoprotein as opportunity-and-barrier themes

What remains thinner is:

- Norwegian deployment metrics at system scale
- operator economics for scaling redistribution and loop pathways
- fermentation commercialization outcomes and scale barriers

That means the current pack no longer has to treat circularity as a blank gap, but it still should not overstate transition maturity. Use `PROJECT-FIT-P2-SYNTHESIS-LAYER.md` as a second-layer chapter input, not as a replacement for the core diagnosis.

## Immediate-core reading order

This is the recommended first-wave sequence for the next synthesis pass.

1. `Konkurransetilsynets Dagligvarerapport 2024-25`
   Why first: best structural baseline for concentration, margins, barriers, and vertical integration.

2. `NorgesGruppens års- og bærekraftsrapport 2024`
   Why second: shows how control is operationalized inside the leading Norwegian operator.

3. `Matsikkerhet og beredskap på landbruksområdet`
   Why third: defines the Norwegian preparedness problem in audit form.

4. `Årsrapport for Dagligvaretilsynet 2024`
   Why fourth: shows how the current enforcement model performs in practice.

5. `Bærekraft i ASKO 2024`
   Why fifth: turns abstract logistics power into physical infrastructure and HORECA reach.

6. `Livsmedelsberedskap för en ny tid`
   Why sixth: adds the best Nordic whole-system preparedness comparator.

7. `Utredning av funksjonelt og regnskapsmessig skille i verdikjeden for mat og dagligvarer`
   Why seventh: frames what transparency and separation reforms could realistically target.

8. `Scenarioanalyse av norsk jordbruk i 2050 med vekt på forholdet mellom kosthold, utslipp, import og matproduksjon`
   Why eighth: gives the future trade-off spine for climate, diet, and resilience.

9. `Konkurransetilsynets marginstudie 2024 — Del 2: Kartlegging av marginer ved bruk av informasjon på produktnivå`
   Why ninth: shows how value capture works inside categories and assortments.

10. `Samarbeidsklimaet i dagligvarebransjen for mindre lokalmat- og drikkeprodusenter 2025`
    Why tenth: connects structure directly to the viability of smaller place-based producers.

11. `Reitan Retail Annual and Sustainability Report 2024`
    Why eleventh: adds a broader Nordic operator model with discount, convenience, and data layers.

12. `Ikkje lett å bli klok på sjølvforsyning`
    Why twelfth: keeps the resilience analysis methodologically honest.

13. `Salling Group Annual Report 2024`
    Why thirteenth: strengthens the Denmark operator comparison.

14. `Importvernets påvirkning på konsentrasjonen i leverandørleddet`
    Why fourteenth: directly ties trade policy to domestic concentration.

15. `Environmental Footprint of the Food Saved by Too Good To Go`
    Why fifteenth: useful method note for avoided-impact claims, but not as foundational as the strongest audits and operator anchors.

16. `Riksrevisjonens undersøkelse av kvotesystemet i kyst- og havfisket`
    Why sixteenth: brings in the marine-governance side of territorial resilience.

17. `Dagligvaretilsynet - tildelingsbrev for 2026`
    Why seventeenth: should be used once the governance baseline is already understood, as the clearest near-term institutional-transition source.
