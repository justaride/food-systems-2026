# Policy-Market Track Note

This track matters for WP3 because the Food Systems 2026 whitepaper needs a defensible account of who controls market access, how the current retail governance model actually works, and which policy levers could open space for circular business models, local producers, and new entrants. These sources are the core evidence base for the whitepaper chapters on market concentration, actor power, and policy recommendations, and they help distinguish structural constraints from downstream symptoms like weak supplier margins or poor assortment.

## Immediate core

### Konkurransetilsynets Dagligvarerapport 2024-25
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Konkurransetilsynets-Dagligvarerapport-2024-25.pdf`

This is the freshest official baseline on the Norwegian grocery market. It brings together market shares, margins, import protection, real-estate barriers, and vertical integration, and frames them as persistent competition problems rather than a temporary inflation episode. It is also useful because it ties the current structure to concrete enforcement and policy issues, including the aftermath of the Price Hunter case and the continued importance of local entry barriers.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Anchor the market-concentration chapter and cite it when explaining why retail structure constrains supplier diversity, circular transition, and new market entry.

### Årsrapport for Dagligvaretilsynet 2024
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Aarsrapport-Dagligvaretilsynet-2024.pdf`

The annual report shows how the current governance model performs in practice, and the picture is mixed. Formal cases and decisions remain few, so the inspectorate relied heavily on guidance, contract-template review, survey work, joint-marketing scrutiny, and outreach to keep the law relevant. The report is especially important because it openly states that suppliers are still reluctant to bring forward concrete cases, partly because they fear harming relationships with one of the very few chains they depend on.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to assess what the current enforcement model actually achieves, where it is preventive rather than coercive, and why institutional design matters for supplier confidence.

### Dagligvaretilsynet - tildelingsbrev for 2026
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Dagligvaretilsynet-tildelingsbrev-2026.pdf`

This is the clearest formal signal on near-term governance change. It states that the Storting has decided to close Dagligvaretilsynet and transfer responsibility for the good-trading-practice law to Konkurransetilsynet, while also instructing the agency to keep enforcing the law, continue collaboration-climate surveys, and document lessons during the transition. That makes it more than an administrative letter: it is the operational bridge between one regulatory model and the next.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Cite it as the current policy baseline for enforcement reform and institutional transition in 2026.

### Utredning av funksjonelt og regnskapsmessig skille i verdikjeden for mat og dagligvarer
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/utredning-av-funksjonelt-og-regnskapsmessig-skille-i-verdikjeden-for-mat-og-dagligvarer.pdf`

Menon and Kvale map the core vertical-integration issues in Norwegian grocery: grossist access, supplier access, EMV, information flows, purchasing-price differences, attractive premises, and transparency. Their conclusion is that current evidence does not justify imposing functional or accounting separation now, but the real value of the report is the structured map of where the pressure points actually are and what legal routes would be available if government wants to act later. It is therefore strongest as a framework and policy-design document, not as a new empirical market study.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to weigh separation and transparency proposals carefully, showing where evidence is strong, where it is contested, and what a realistic regulatory pathway would look like.

### Konkurransetilsynets marginstudie 2024 — Del 2: Kartlegging av marginer ved bruk av informasjon på produktnivå
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Del-2.-Kartlegging-av-marginer-ved-bruk-av-informasjon-pa-produktniva.pdf`

This is the best product-level evidence in the shortlist. It shows that value capture differs sharply by category, with high-margin “cash cow” categories alongside traffic-driver categories that are sold on very low or even negative margins. It also shows that chains were better than suppliers at passing through cost increases during the inflationary period, and that EMV can matter strategically even when their per-unit margins are lower than branded products. That makes the report useful for understanding not just average profitability but how pricing power is distributed through the assortment.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to explain category-level price strategy, supplier squeeze, and where circular or local offerings are likely to face the hardest commercial terms.

### Samarbeidsklimaet i dagligvarebransjen for mindre lokalmat- og drikkeprodusenter 2025
- `tier`: `immediate-core`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Rapport+om+samarbeidsklimaet+lokalmat-+og+drikke+produsenter+i+dagligvarebransjen+2025.pdf`

This is the rare source in the batch that looks directly at small local food and drink producers rather than large national suppliers. It finds a worse collaboration climate than the main supplier survey, low knowledge of both the law and the regulator, frequent absence of written agreements, and real difficulty understanding contract structures designed for much larger counterparties. The report is especially important because it ties these frictions to the government’s own ambition to grow local-food turnover, meaning the policy goal and the market-access reality are not yet aligned.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as the clearest bridge between market structure, producer diversity, and the practical barriers facing smaller and more place-based food businesses.

## Core support

### Dagligvare: Forskjeller i innkjøpspriser ytterligere redusert
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Dagligvare_ Forskjeller i innkjøpspriser ytterligere redusert - Konkurransetilsynet.pdf`

This note matters because it shows both change and continuity. Purchasing-price gaps between the chains have narrowed since 2017, but NorgesGruppen still secures systematically better terms than Coop and Rema 1000, which keeps buyer-power asymmetry in place even after some convergence. It also helps interpret those gaps by linking them to services, campaigns, and bargaining structure rather than pretending list prices alone explain the power balance.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it when explaining why scale, logistics, and bargaining position still shape supplier economics and challenger viability even if the headline gap has narrowed.

### Utredning om prisjusteringsvinduer 2023
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Utredning-om-prisjusteringsvinduer-2023.pdf`

This is one of the most concrete descriptions of a market rule that likely distorted competition. It explains the Norwegian system of fixed pricing windows, shows that consumer prices historically moved around those windows more than supplier prices did, and argues that the arrangement benefited large established actors and dampened genuine price competition. It also documents that the system began to unravel in 2023, making it useful both as diagnosis and as a marker of structural change.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as a worked example of how industry routines and bargaining calendars can shape prices and margins as much as formal regulation does.

### Egne merkevarer (EMV) og innovasjon i dagligvare
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/2022-68-EMV-og-innovasjon-i-dagligvare-1.pdf`

This report is valuable precisely because it is not a neutral authority document. Menon argues that EMV can stimulate innovation, give smaller producers market access, and remain less dominant in Norway than in many European markets, but the study was commissioned by Virke Dagligvare and therefore reflects a chain-friendly framing of the issue. Read together with the more neutral SØA mapping and the margin work, it becomes a useful counterweight rather than a standalone conclusion.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to avoid an overly simple anti-EMV story and to show where the debate is genuinely contested between competition, innovation, and dependency concerns.

### Forbyr praksis som motvirker konkurranse i dagligvaremarkedet
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Forbyr praksis som motvirker konkurranse i dagligvaremarkedet - regjeringen.no.pdf`

This 2023 press release marks the regulatory turn against negative servitudes. It frames access to good premises as central to grocery competition, cites Konkurransetilsynet’s finding of more than 350 registered servitudes favoring the big three, and makes clear that government sees this as deliberate exclusion rather than legitimate competition. It also matters because it separates two issues that are often conflated: negative servitudes were banned, while exclusive leases were left for further mapping.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as the clearest short-form source on how real-estate control became an active policy issue in Norwegian grocery competition.

### Nye tiltak for bedre konkurranse i dagligvarebransjen
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Nye tiltak for bedre konkurranse i dagligvarebransjen - regjeringen.no.pdf`

This is the live reform package rather than historical background. It identifies four active policy workstreams: better price information and oversight of unit pricing and loyalty schemes, action on exclusive leases, an updated good-trading-practice law, and a review of functional or accounting separation to improve transparency. The press release is especially helpful because it names EMV, rebates, enforcement design, and opaque corporate structures as current policy problems rather than abstract academic concerns.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to show which reforms are already politically live, and which WP3 recommendations would extend existing government work versus require a new direction.

### Rapport om samarbeidsklimaet i dagligvarebransjen 2024
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Rapport+om+samarbeidsklimaet+i+dagligvarebransjen+2024_Final_17.10.24.pdf`

The 2024 collaboration-climate survey is the most useful pre-2025 baseline in the shortlist. It shows gradual improvement and somewhat smaller differences between chains, but still points to room for better negotiation efficiency and better follow-up in practice. Because the survey methodology also changed, it is useful both as evidence and as a reminder to be careful about over-reading small year-on-year movements.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as the baseline in a short time series with the 2025 survey and the localmat variant, especially when discussing whether the governance regime had any normalizing effect before the institutional reorganization.

### Nordic Food Markets — A Taste for Competition
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/NORDIC_FOOD_MARKETS.pdf`

Although it is a 2005 report, it remains the foundational Nordic benchmark for concentration, high prices, narrow assortments, and buyer power. Its real contribution is historical continuity: the central structural problems in today’s Norwegian debate were already clearly identified across Nordic food markets two decades ago. That makes it useful not because it is current, but because it demonstrates how long-lived these competition patterns are.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it to frame today’s Norwegian market issues as durable Nordic structural problems rather than as a short-term post-inflation anomaly.

### Finnish Grocery Trade 2024
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/Paivittaistavarakauppa-ry-2024-EN.pdf`

PTY’s report is the cleanest Finnish sector-statistics source in the shortlist. It gives market shares, store counts, and turnover data that make Finland a strong Nordic comparator, especially because it combines extreme concentration with a different ownership pattern: dominant cooperative S Group, Kesko’s trader model, and Lidl as the only meaningful challenger. That makes it useful for testing whether Norwegian problems are unique or part of a broader Nordic market design.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as the primary Finland comparator when discussing concentration, challenger barriers, and the limits of alternative ownership models.

### Samkeppniseftirlitið Annual Report 2024
- `tier`: `core-support`
- `source_path`: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/03_Policy_Governance_And_Market/SAMKEPPNISEFTIRLITID_arsskyrlsla_2024_LOK_02.10.pdf`

This annual report contributes less direct grocery-market data than the Norwegian and Finnish sources, but it is strong on enforcement capacity and regulatory priorities in a very small market. It shows that the Icelandic authority handled roughly 120 administrative matters in 2024, had around 23 staff, and still named daily consumer markets as a strategic priority for 2025-2027 while warning that limited resources force hard triage. Read together with the Iceland market note already in the library, it becomes a useful comparator for what small-state competition oversight looks like in an import-dependent food system.

`Use in Food Systems 2026:` Use it as a governance-capacity comparator when discussing how much enforcement design and institutional bandwidth matter in highly concentrated food markets.

## Cross-track takeaways

- The strongest common finding is that retail and wholesale structure is not just a competition issue; it shapes whether smaller, local, circular, or otherwise differentiated producers can reach the market at all.
- The most important bottlenecks are access bottlenecks: access to purchasing terms, access to contracts that are understandable and enforceable, access to grossist and logistics channels, and access to attractive premises.
- The Norwegian governance model is in the middle of a real transition, not a stable equilibrium. That matters for WP3 because recommendations on market access, EMV, transparency, and supplier protection have to be read against a moving enforcement architecture.
- Nordic comparator material does not offer a simple “ownership fix.” Norway, Finland, and Iceland all show that high concentration, limited entry, and weak transparency can persist under different ownership structures and regulatory traditions.
- This track also strengthens the bridge to other WP3 evidence: operator reports explain how the large chains use scale, logistics, and property; resilience sources explain why import dependence and concentration matter systemically; the policy-market track explains why those patterns are hard to change.

## Deferred / secondary items

- Exclusive leases remain a real evidence gap compared with negative servitudes. The policy direction is clear, but the public factual base is still much thinner than for tinglyste servitutter.
- If EMV becomes a central whitepaper subsection, add the Samfunnsøkonomisk Analyse EMV / vertical-integration report directly beside Menon so the counter-arguments are balanced with a more state-commissioned baseline.
- The loyalty-program workstream is now active and should be revisited with the 2026 follow-up reports if WP3 wants a stronger consumer-information angle.
- The Iceland comparator is useful, but it should remain secondary unless WP3 needs a small-island or import-dependence governance case specifically; Finland is the cleaner Nordic statistics benchmark.
- Source coverage note: the local markdown layer was strong for Dagligvarerapporten, margin work, EMV, Nordic benchmarks, and several legal syntheses, but I had to read imported PDFs directly for the 2026 tildelingsbrev, the localmat-producer survey, the 2025 reform package, the 2023 servitude announcement, and the 2024 Dagligvaretilsynet annual report because dedicated summaries were thin or absent.
