# Project Fit Track — Operator Evidence

This track matters for WP3 because the whitepaper cannot rely on policy and market-structure sources alone; it also needs operator-side evidence for where control actually sits in practice. These documents make WP3's value-chain analysis, actor-power mapping, and circular business model chapter more concrete by showing how retail scale, wholesale/logistics assets, data systems, supplier governance, and surplus-handling models work on the ground.

The note below uses local summaries first from `research/bibliotek`, `src/lib/data`, and intake artifacts, with selective PDF spot-checks only where title metadata was noisy.

## Immediate core

### NorgesGruppens års- og bærekraftsrapport 2024
Tier: `immediate-core`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/ng_ars--og-barekraftsrapport-2024.pdf`

This is the anchor document for Norwegian operator power. Local summaries show 2024 revenue of NOK 118.0bn, EBIT margin around 4.1%, 43.5% market share, and a portfolio led by Kiwi, while also highlighting EMV growth through First Price and value capture through upstream positions such as ASKO, BAMA, Dagrofa, and Joh. Johannson. It contributes more than financial scale: it shows how retail, wholesale, and supplier-side integration reinforce one another inside the same operator system.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the main operator baseline for Norwegian market power, vertical integration, and the link between margin discipline, EMV strategy, and logistics control.

### Bærekraft i ASKO 2024
Tier: `immediate-core`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/barekraft-i-asko-2024.pdf`

This is the clearest physical distribution document in the shortlist. The local synthesis quantifies ASKO's reach across grocery and servering/HORECA with 1.1 million deliveries, 15,626 customers, 223 fossil-free trucks, 95 GWh own renewable energy, and large-scale donation and side-stream handling of surplus food. It turns "logistics power" from an abstract thesis into visible infrastructure, fleet, warehouse, and redistribution capacity.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to document wholesale and logistics as a decisive control point, and to connect retail concentration to HORECA reach, energy transition, and operational food-waste handling.

### Reitan Retail Annual and Sustainability Report 2024
Tier: `immediate-core`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/Reitan Retail 2025_Oppslag.pdf`

This is the strongest cross-Nordic counterpart to NorgesGruppen because it places REMA 1000 Norway, REMA 1000 Denmark, convenience, mobility, real estate, and sustainability governance inside one reporting frame. Local summaries and a first-page PDF check show a 2024 "Better together" annual and sustainability report with group revenue around NOK 109.3bn, systemwide sales around NOK 132.1bn, and full ESRS-based sustainability sections, while the internal summary layer adds the strategic importance of Danish expansion and the customer-data ecosystem around `Æ`. It therefore contributes a broader operator model than pure grocery retail alone.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the main case for discount-led Nordic expansion, digital demand-side leverage, and how a food operator can scale through adjacent formats and geographies.

### Salling Group Annual Report 2024
Tier: `immediate-core`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/Annual report 2024.pdf`

This is the Denmark anchor for operator evidence. The local summaries show a 72.2bn DKK business with 1,770 stores, strong format differentiation across Netto, føtex, Bilka, and Salling, and a 2024 strategy cycle built around `Aspire '28`, supplier climate expectations, and `MadPlus` as a food-waste initiative. It is especially valuable because it sits directly beside aggressive consolidation logic: the Coop Danmark store acquisition and the planned Rimi Baltic expansion.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to show how operator strategy, store formats, supplier requirements, and M&A combine in a high-scale Nordic retail model.

### Environmental Footprint of the Food Saved by Too Good To Go
Tier: `immediate-core`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/2024 Environmental Footprint Report ENG.pdf`

This is not a conventional retailer annual report, which is exactly why it matters in the operator track. A local PDF spot-check shows a clear scenario methodology comparing disposal of surplus food with human consumption through Too Good To Go, estimating net avoided climate impact at roughly `-2.65 kg CO2e/kg` surplus food saved with land-use change included, while also showing that transport offsets more than 10% of avoided impacts and that displacement assumptions matter materially. It contributes reusable measurement logic for redistribution claims rather than only platform growth rhetoric.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the best ready-made method note for avoided-impact accounting in surplus-food pilots, redistribution metrics, and circular business model evaluation.

## Core support

### Kesko Annual Report 2024
Tier: `core-support`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/kesko-annual-report-2024.pdf`

This is the strongest Finland retail anchor in the shortlist. Local summaries show a hybrid national model built around roughly 860 K-retailers, about 1,100 grocery stores, a strong grocery margin, extensive digital grocery coverage, and a major foodservice position through Kespro, alongside continuing logistics and hypermarket buildout. It contributes a useful contrast to Norway by showing that national scale does not require the same level of fully centralized operator structure.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to compare Finnish hybrid ownership, grocery-foodservice integration, and logistics expansion with the more vertically integrated Norwegian and Danish cases.

### Matsmart Års- och hållbarhetsredovisning 2024
Tier: `core-support`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/Matsmart-Ars-och-hallbarhetsredovisning-2024.pdf`

Matsmart is the clearest commercial surplus-resale case in the shortlist. A local first-page check plus the alternative-distribution synthesis show a company shifting from growth-first expansion to a more durable profit model, with Swedish profitability, improved EBITDA, cost cuts, automated warehousing, and a business that benefits from both consumer price sensitivity and suppliers' need to move surplus stock. It contributes a pragmatic view of circular commerce as disciplined retail operations, not only mission-led communications.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as a support case for scaleable surplus resale, especially where circularity depends on inventory management, pricing, and warehouse economics rather than donation alone.

### Cheffelo Årsredovisning & Hållbarhetsrapport 2025
Tier: `core-support`  
Source_path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/06_Company_And_Annual_Reports/Cheffelo_Arsredovisning_Hallbarhetsrapport_2025_SE.pdf`

Cheffelo gives the operator track a demand-planning case instead of a pure retail or rescue case. A local first-page check and the alternative-distribution synthesis show a profitable Scandinavian meal-kit operator with 2025 revenue of about SEK 1.188bn, roughly 17 million meals delivered, own production facilities, and an explicit promise of minimal food waste through planned portions and close supplier coordination. That makes the report useful for understanding how menu design, procurement, packaging, and last-mile logistics can reduce waste upstream of the store shelf.

Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as a support case for planned-demand business models, especially when assessing whether circularity can be built into household food access before surplus emerges.

## Cross-track takeaways

- The operator reports confirm that control in Nordic food systems sits in integrated systems of retail, wholesale, logistics, data, and upstream supplier coordination, not in the store layer alone.
- The most useful circular cases in this track are operational, not rhetorical: ASKO on redistribution, Too Good To Go on avoided-impact methodology, Matsmart on surplus resale economics, and Cheffelo on planned-demand reduction of waste.
- The Nordic comparison is strongest when ownership model and infrastructure are read together: Norway leans toward tightly integrated operator systems, Finland shows more hybrid merchant structure, and Denmark shows aggressive format management plus consolidation.
- This track should be read alongside policy-market and resilience-security, because supplier governance, logistics chokepoints, and import-sensitive infrastructure are where market power and system resilience meet.
