# Project Fit Track: Resilience / Security

This track matters for WP3 because resilience and security turn food-system structure into a concrete public-risk question: can Norway still produce, move, and distribute enough food when trade, energy, logistics, biology, or geopolitics fail? For WP3, these sources are especially useful because they connect preparedness to market concentration, import dependence, production incentives, and the practical capacity of both land-based and marine food systems.

## Immediate-core

### Matsikkerhet og beredskap på landbruksområdet
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/matsikkerhet-og-beredskap-pa-landbruksomradet.pdf`
- Summary: This is the clearest Norwegian baseline diagnosis of food preparedness gaps. Riksrevisjonen concludes that authorities are not sufficiently prepared for serious crises affecting food security, and highlights weak contingency planning, excessive reliance on normal international trade, and underuse of domestic production potential in grain and vegetables. It is the strongest single source for moving the discussion from abstract “self-sufficiency” targets to operational preparedness failures.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the core Norwegian problem statement for WP3’s resilience framing and as the benchmark against which newer policy responses should be judged.

### Livsmedelsberedskap för en ny tid
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/livsmedelsberedskap-for-en-ny-tid-sou-20248.pdf`
- Summary: The Swedish preparedness model is valuable because it treats food security as part of total defence rather than only an agricultural output question. The note shows how Sweden assigns responsibilities across national agencies, regions, municipalities, business, and civil society, while explicitly including fuel, power, water, payments, and logistics in food preparedness. It also makes clear that storage is only one instrument inside a broader governance and distribution system.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the best Nordic comparator for a whole-system preparedness model that goes beyond self-sufficiency percentages and toward governance, logistics, and local delivery capacity.

### Scenarioanalyse av norsk jordbruk i 2050 med vekt på forholdet mellom kosthold, utslipp, import og matproduksjon
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/Mittenzwei-mfl-2026-Scenarioanalyse-av-norsk-jordbruk-i-2050.pdf`
- Summary: This report models two long-run transition paths for Norwegian agriculture: one based on dietary-guideline compliance and one based on maximizing domestic resource use to reach 50 percent self-sufficiency corrected for imported feed. It quantifies effects on land use, imports, food production, value creation, regional distribution, and greenhouse-gas emissions, and it is explicit that major changes in farming, processing, and consumption would be required under either path. The contribution is not a single answer, but a structured map of trade-offs WP3 needs to explain.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to frame scenario logic and to show that resilience, climate, and diet cannot be treated as separate workstreams.

### Ikkje lett å bli klok på sjølvforsyning
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/Ikkje lett å bli klok på sjølvforsyning - Nibio.pdf`
- Summary: This is the necessary methodological correction to any resilience argument built around headline self-sufficiency numbers. The underlying NIBIO material shows that the result depends heavily on what is counted, whether imported feed is corrected for, whether one measures energy or weight, and whether the question is present consumption or crisis diet capacity. It is especially useful for exposing hidden dependencies on feed, machinery, spare parts, labour, and imported inputs that disappear in simplified public debate.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to keep WP3 analytically disciplined and to avoid building the resilience case on a single, politically convenient percentage.

### Importvernets påvirkning på konsentrasjonen i leverandørleddet
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/importvern_til_godkjenning---sensurert-adobe.pdf`
- Summary: This NHH master thesis is one of the clearest local analyses linking trade protection to upstream concentration in the grocery value chain. It argues that high tariff protection reduces outside options, helps keep Norwegian products as the cheapest viable alternative, and reinforces strong supplier positions in already concentrated categories. That makes it important for WP3 because resilience is not only about import exposure; it is also about how policy design shapes concentration and bargaining power inside the domestic system.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to connect import policy, supplier concentration, and system resilience in the same analytical frame rather than treating them as separate policy domains.

### Riksrevisjonens undersøkelse av kvotesystemet i kyst- og havfisket
- Tier: `immediate-core`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/kvotesystemet_i_kyst_og_havfisket.pdf`
- Summary: Local structured coverage for this exact source is weak, so this summary relies on the intake PDF itself plus the broader resilience context in `research/bibliotek/akademia/internasjonalt/matsikkerhet-beredskap-global.md`. The audit finds that quota-system changes increased profitability in the fleet, but also challenged long-standing fisheries policy principles, raised quota prices, made recruitment harder, and weakened activity in parts of the smallest coastal fleet and several fishery-dependent municipalities. For WP3, its value is that it makes the marine side of resilience concrete: concentrated resource control can improve efficiency while reducing recruitment, territorial spread, and local food-system robustness.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the key marine-governance source for showing that resilience is partly about distributed production and landing capacity, not only aggregate export success.

## Core-support

### Riksrevisjonens undersøking om reduksjon av klimagassutslepp frå jordbruket
- Tier: `core-support`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/reduksjon-av-klimagassutslepp-fra-jordbruket.pdf`
- Summary: Local structured coverage for this exact document is weak, so the summary is based on the intake PDF. The audit concludes that there are no clear signs Norway will achieve major agricultural emissions reductions by 2030, that current policy instruments are too weak, and that responsibility is poorly aligned across agriculture, climate, health, diet, and food-waste policy. It matters for WP3 because it shows that any serious resilience strategy must also solve the climate side of agricultural transition rather than treating preparedness and decarbonization as separate agendas.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to anchor the climate-resilience trade-off and to argue that credible preparedness policy must also work under emissions constraints.

### Milepæl for norsk beredskap
- Tier: `core-support`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/Milepæl for norsk beredskap - regjeringen.no.pdf`
- Summary: This is a government news page rather than a deep analysis, but it is still a useful operational proof-point. It documents that the state signed long-run contracts with four grain-sector actors to build a national emergency reserve for food grain, with an initial 30,000 tonnes and a plan to scale toward three months of flour wheat consumption by 2029. The piece shows preparedness moving from rhetoric into contracts, ownership, storage design, and physical distribution arrangements.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as the clearest current example of preparedness policy becoming concrete infrastructure, while noting that it addresses only one part of a wider resilience challenge.

### Myndighetenes arbeid med fiskehelse og fiskevelferd i havbruksnæringen
- Tier: `core-support`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/myndighetenes-arbeid-med-fiskehelse-og-fiskevelferd-i-havbruksnaringen.pdf`
- Summary: Local structured coverage for this exact audit is weak, so the summary relies on the intake PDF, supported by `research/bibliotek/akademia/andre-norske/nofima-mattrygghet-sjomat-prosessering.md`. The audit identifies persistent disease, high mortality, fragmented regulation, weak risk management requirements, underdeveloped risk-based supervision, and insufficient coordination between authorities. For WP3, it is useful because it reframes seafood not as a guaranteed resilience asset, but as a biologically fragile system whose reliability depends on governance and animal-health performance.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use to qualify overly simple “Norway is secure because we have seafood” arguments and to make biological robustness part of the marine resilience analysis.

### Norsk sjømatnæring – fra subsidiesluk til pengemaskin
- Tier: `core-support`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/Norsk sjømatnæring – fra subsidiesluk til pengemaskin _ Nofima.pdf`
- Summary: This source is weaker than the others because it is a Nofima web feature built around Audun Iversen’s doctoral work, not a stand-alone analytical report; the closest structured local support is `research/bibliotek/akademia/andre-norske/nofima-mattrygghet-sjomat-prosessering.md`. Even so, it contributes a useful macro frame: Norwegian seafood has become a major export and value-creation engine driven by aquaculture innovation, while fisheries and land-based processing still face structural pressure. For WP3, the article helps explain the blue-sector paradox: Norway can be a seafood powerhouse and still have unresolved domestic resilience issues in land food supply, distribution, and food security.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as contextual framing for the marine side of the resilience story, but do not treat it as a substitute for a harder policy or audit source.

### Matkjedene skviser bonden
- Tier: `core-support`
- Source path: `Food Research Process 20.04.26/08_Food_Security_Agriculture_And_Seafood/Matkjedene skviser bonden _ Framtiden i våre hender.pdf`
- Summary: This is an advocacy source rather than a neutral sector report, and local structured coverage for the exact item is weak. Still, it is a useful current illustration of how retailer power can erode domestic production capacity: the piece argues that recent food-price increases did not reach farmers, highlights freeze-rebate dynamics in meat, and shows how non-binding purchasing arrangements and chain power can block greener and more domestic-oriented production. It therefore adds a concrete political-economy layer to the resilience debate: preparedness depends not only on how much Norway can produce, but on whether producers can afford and dare to produce it.
- Use in Food Systems 2026: Use as a current pressure signal on producer viability and market access, while keeping its advocacy bias explicit.

## Cross-track takeaways

- The strongest pattern across the track is that resilience is not just a stock-or-self-sufficiency issue; it is a governance, logistics, and coordination issue spanning trade, energy, payment systems, disease management, and local distribution.
- Multiple sources challenge the idea that a single self-sufficiency metric can stand in for preparedness. Feed dependence, imported inputs, crisis diets, and distribution bottlenecks all matter.
- The land and marine systems show a similar tension: concentration and scale can improve efficiency and profitability, but may also reduce territorial spread, recruitment, redundancy, and crisis robustness.
- The most useful WP3 framing is therefore not “produce more at any cost,” but “build a food system that can still function under stress while remaining economically viable, climate-relevant, and distributively fair.”
